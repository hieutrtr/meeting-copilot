### Per-call cost projection (for downstream sanity)

The settings store runs entirely in-process, no SDK calls — **$0.000000 / call**. Its overhead per setter is:

1. Validation (`isFiniteNumber` / `clamp`) — 2 comparisons.
2. `set(...)` — 1 zustand state-merge call (constant time on 5-field state).
3. `persistToStorage` — 1 `JSON.stringify` (sub-microsecond on 5 primitive fields) + 1 `setItem` call.

Worst-case worker overhead per setter call < 50 µs. Setter call frequency is bounded by the user's UI interaction rate (≤ 10/min on a slider drag, much less in steady state) — negligible vs. ARCH §12 latency budget. The composer wrapper `classifyWithSettingsGate` adds 1 boolean check + 1 await + 1 numeric comparison on top of `guardedClassifyWithHaiku`'s overhead — also sub-millisecond.

### Constant verification — single source of truth

The store imports tunable defaults from their owning modules instead of redefining them:

- `HAIKU_ADMIT_THRESHOLD` from `src/llm/haikuFilter.ts` (T-2.3)
- `DEFAULT_SILENCE_MS` from `src/detector/sliding-window.ts` (T-2.2; export added in this task)
- `DEFAULT_THRESHOLD_USD_PER_HOUR` from `src/cost/costGuard.ts` (T-2.7)

Drift between store defaults and the source-of-truth constants would surface in `SS-S1` (defaults verbatim) test on the next run. This anchors the audit trail to exactly three places — re-tuning Haiku admit threshold edits the constant in `haikuFilter.ts` and the store reads it on the next construction, no second-source maintenance needed.
